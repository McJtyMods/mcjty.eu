# Ariente

## Introduction

Ariente combines alien technology with exploration. Build machines, automate a
workshop, customize powered armor and weapons, and travel by flux-powered vehicles.
With ArienteWorld installed, you earn that technology by exploring an alien
dimension and conquering its defended complexes.

The two mods have different roles:

| Mod | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| [Ariente on CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/ariente) | Machines, dual-flux power, blueprints, automation, storage, equipment, vehicles and building blocks. Can be played on its own. |
| [Ariente World on CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/ariente-world) | The dimension, cities, dungeons, alien inhabitants and exploration progression. Requires Ariente. |

This guide covers the current ports for Minecraft **1.20.1 and later**. The
original **1.12.2 alpha releases** do not include all these features. Choose
downloads for the same Minecraft version and loader. Install the dependencies
listed for your files, including
[McJtyLib](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/mcjtylib) and
[HoloGui](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/hologui).

**JEI** is useful for looking up recipes and their required blueprints. Where a
compatible **Patchouli** version is available, the craftable **Ariente Tablet**
opens an in-game manual. Machine holograms also have a question-mark button linking
to the relevant entry when the manual is available. The Tablet needs no fuel.

Numbers below describe default settings; modpacks and server settings can change
recipes, fuel costs, generation and difficulty.

## Getting started

With both mods installed, a useful first route is:

1. Make Ariente Pearls and find an underground Overworld entrance.
2. Charge its Warper and enter Ariente. Mark the return Warper before exploring.
3. Gather minerals and raid smaller complexes for blueprints and equipment.
4. Craft a Constructor and Blueprint Storage, then use your discoveries to build
   a workshop.
5. Establish both flux supplies, automate production, and improve your gear.
6. Take on larger installations, flying scouts and eventually the huge cube and
   its Core Guardian.

Bring food, blocks, a pickaxe, good weapons, armor and spare inventory space.
Even small bases have dangerous defenders; an unarmored player can die to one
soldier's sabre hit, including on Easy difficulty.

### Playing without ArienteWorld

Ariente can also provide technology for an ordinary Overworld base. Without
ArienteWorld, default settings enable Ariente ores in newly generated Overworld
terrain and add blueprint rewards to supported structure chests. Search villages,
shipwrecks, ocean ruins, buried treasure, strongholds and other vanilla structures.
These rewards include starter machine or component patterns as well as random
blueprints.

Gather Silver, Platinum, Silicon and marble for your first Constructor and
Blueprint Storage. From there, the same workshop systems apply. An extremely rare
Extreme Circuit blueprint provides an alternative to the dimension's boss reward.

## Holographic controls

Most machines use a hologram in the world instead of a full-screen inventory.
Stand in front of it and aim at the actual control.

| Action | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Open a machine's hologram | Right-click the machine. |
| Press a button or toggle | Aim at it and left-click. |
| Craft or transfer items in most machine panels | Double-click the displayed slot. Hover first to read its tooltip. |
| Adjust a numeric setting | Click or drag its slider, or use the arrows. |
| Keep a block panel open while looking away | Click its pushpin; a green upright pin means pinned. |
| Close a panel | Click X. |
| Configure worn power armor or a held modular weapon | Press top-row **0** by default. |
| Toggle assigned equipment groups | Numpad **7** and **8** by default; two more groups can be bound in Controls. |

Storage Cubes and Holo Storage Cubes have their own transfer controls, described
below. Check Minecraft's Controls menu if equipment or vehicle bindings conflict
with another mod.

## Exploring ArienteWorld

### Overworld entrance and Warpers {#overworld-dungeon}

Surround an Ender Pearl with eight Nether Quartz at a crafting table to make an
**Ariente Pearl**. Use it in the air in the Overworld to send a locator toward a
nearby intact entrance. Follow it, then search underground. New entrance rooms
prefer dry cave connections, sometimes with a short tunnel, but some remain
buried. If no entrance is found, try another area. Thrown pearls can be lost, so
bring extras.

Inside the entrance, right-click the **Warper** with pearls to charge it. The
default requirement is **10 pearls**. Open its hologram and press **Warp** once
charged. This charge is an unlock requirement: ordinary trips do not consume it.
Cable power does not charge a Warper.

The arrival building in Ariente has an already charged return Warper. Record its
location and keep a safe route back. Returning first tries your remembered
Overworld entrance; if that landing is unusable, it falls back to a valid respawn
position or world spawn.

### Custom dimension

Ariente has marble landscapes, floating terrain, alien trees, oceans, swamps and
city districts beneath a purple sky. Rough biomes contain Negarite and Posirite
crystal formations: a stone pickaxe or better gives matching fuel dust, while Silk
Touch collects the crystals themselves. Forests and swamps include blue spiral
trees and unusual plants.

Explore sprawling street districts, underground installations, floating
ship-shaped complexes and a huge surface cube. Stations and beam tunnels form
another transport network. Self-contained complexes have organized defenses,
security systems and AI Cores; their difficulty varies considerably.

New terrain and structure additions appear in newly generated chunks. Existing
explored buildings do not acquire every new room or reward.

### Mining and materials

Take a stone pickaxe or better for the resource ores. Typical deposit heights in
the Ariente dimension are:

| Resource | Typical Y range | Main use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Negarite and Posirite | 6–29 | Fuel dust for generators and equipment, plus crafting. |
| Lithium | 8–19 | Advanced components and equipment. |
| Silver | 10–19 | Common machine ingredients. |
| Manganese | 20–49 | Circuits and machinery. |
| Silicon | 30–59 | Electronics. |
| Platinum | 30–79 | Equipment and advanced machines. |
| Lapis and Glowstone | 10–29 | Crafting and lighting. |

Veins can extend beyond these ranges. Overworld ore distributions are different.
Silver, Platinum, Lithium and Manganese drop raw metals: smelt or blast them into
ingots. Fortune increases raw-metal and Silicon drops; Silk Touch preserves ore
blocks. Negarite and Posirite ores drop their blocks, each yielding **four dust**
when smelted or blasted. Keep both fuel types.

### Defenders and alerts

Soldiers fight with sabres or two-handed Energy Lances. Combat Drones attack from
the air; Sentinel Drones watch for intruders and raise alerts. Master Soldiers are
tougher, regenerate, and resist fire and magic. Advanced bases have lasers that
can kill through plain netherite armor in one hit. Use cover, enchantments and
powered protection before challenging them.

Detection and attacks can put a complex on alert, triggering reinforcements and
forcefields. Further sightings keep the alarm active. Break line of sight and
retreat to let it settle. The Alarm panels show **Safe**, **Alert** or **Dead**.

Natural soldier patrols are separate from these reinforcements. They can spawn
inside dungeons and city biomes at light levels of **11 or lower**, even after the
AI is defeated. Lighting rooms to 12 or higher stops those natural spawns, but
does not stop fixed guards or AI reinforcements.

Outside cities and dungeon grounds, rare **Darkbats** circle and dive at players.
They attack even when you are well rested and do not burn in daylight. Solid
cover helps against their dives.

### Flying scouts

Larger operational bases launch soldier-piloted Flux Ships. A scout that sees you
can break from its patrol, line up its front cannon and make repeated firing
passes. Roofs and walls block its sight and shots; staying hidden for a full
minute lets it resume its route.

The hull is indestructible, but laser hits on it pass part of their damage to the
pilot. Defeat the pilot and the ship descends, allowing you to recover it.
Destroying a base's last AI Core prevents new scout launches from that base.

### Conquering a complex

Destroy **every AI Core** in a complex to defeat its organized defense. The final
core drops a Key Card with that city's **storage access**. Hold it and right-click
matching locked Storage Cubes to unlock them, then put the card away to use the
storage normally.

An AI Core takes about **10 seconds to mine with an unenchanted netherite
pickaxe**. Tool enchantments and other mining modifiers still apply. Secure cover
before committing to breaking one.

Surviving enemies remain, and natural patrols can still spawn. Captured generators
also need your fuel once their remaining dust runs out. Loot and fixed defenders
do not replenish simply because you leave and return.

Soldiers can drop **door-access** or **forcefield-immunity** cards; some dropped
cards have no access permission. **Storage access** comes from the final AI Core.
These permissions are separate: a city name alone does not mean a card opens
everything. Keep useful cards until you have checked what they do.

### The Core Guardian

The huge cube's central core is protected by a **Core Guardian**, a hovering
energy sphere with a boss bar. The core cannot be mined or destroyed by explosions
until the guardian dies. Use cover to interrupt its charged lightning attacks.
Ordinary armor, even Protection IV netherite, cannot keep a player at normal
maximum health alive through a direct strike. Bring a full powered suit with
working Armor Upgrades, a Forcefield module and reliable fuel.

Defeating the guardian awards **one Extreme Circuit**. Then break the core for the
storage card and claim the nearby Holo Storage Cube's Overworld spoils, including
netherite, Nether Stars, enchanted equipment and other valuables. The guardian and
these rewards do not respawn.

## Blueprints and construction

Most technology is made from **populated blueprints**, rather than directly at a
crafting table. Blueprints show their result and ingredients and are reusable.
Blank blueprints teach no recipe. With ArienteWorld, caches are the main source;
armor and weapon blueprints are more common than their matching finished items.

### Your first Constructor

The starter **Constructor** and **Blueprint Storage** have ordinary crafting-table
recipes, so you do not need another machine to build them.

1. Put Blueprint Storage directly against any face of the Constructor.
2. Open the storage hologram and transfer populated blueprints into it. Each
   storage block holds six; more adjacent blocks expand the library.
3. Carry the recipe ingredients in your player inventory.
4. Open the Constructor, hover over a result to check requirements, and
   double-click its blueprint to craft.

The manual Constructor needs **no flux**. Results go into your inventory; leave
room for them. JEI can show the blueprint, ingredients and full output for a
Constructor recipe.

### Automatic crafting and spare blueprints

The **Auto Constructor** uses the same adjacent blueprint library, but consumes
ingredients from its own inventory and needs both flux types. Load ingredients
through its hologram or item automation and collect products from its output.
Check its redstone mode. It crafts available recipes from the library; you must
provide subcomponents yourself.

Combine **three valid blueprints** in a crafting grid to consume them and receive
one different pattern. Blueprint tooltips show Common, Uncommon, Rare or Extreme
rarity. Input rarity influences the result, with a chance to move up or down a
tier. Duplicate patterns are useful exchange material.

## Dual power system

Ariente machines use **Negarite flux and Posirite flux together**. A requirement
of 100 means 100 of each. Extra Negarite cannot replace missing Posirite, so size
your workshop around the weaker supply.

### Generators and wiring

1. Place one Negarite Generator and one Posirite Generator.
2. Stack at least one matching tank directly above each generator, without gaps.
3. Load matching fuel dust through the hologram arrows or item automation.
4. Run each colored cable network to a matching **Connector touching the machine**.
   The two connectors can touch different faces of the same machine.

Each matching tank adds **1,000 flux per tick** of its generator's type. More tanks
increase output. A dust item normally burns for about **30 seconds** while the
generator is enabled, even if you are not using its output. Use the redstone-mode
control to turn idle generators off.

An ordinary cable carries power along the route; a Connector supplies the
consumer. If a fueled generator runs but a machine does not, check its tanks,
connectors, both flux supplies and the machine's redstone mode.

Flux networks provide a continuing supply, with no general battery reserve from
unused production. Some machines accumulate a small operating reserve. The
**Flux Capacitor** is a crafting component, and the **Energy Holder** fuels
equipment; neither acts as a placeable network battery. Ariente flux is separate
from Forge Energy/RF.

### Combined cables and facades

A **Power Combiner** accepts separate Negarite and Posirite supplies and outputs
both through Combined Connectors and Combined Cable. Set its transfer limit in
the hologram to cover your machines' demand. Combination normally loses **2%** of
each flux. Merely touching different cable colors together does not combine them.

To hide wiring, use a **Facade** on a normal block to copy its appearance, then
apply it to a cable or connector. Breaking covered wiring first removes the
facade, exposing the cable underneath.

## Processing and machine upgrades

| Machine | How to use it |
| --- | --- |
| **Ore Purifier** | Build a straight horizontal line: Purifier Start, one to four tubes, Purifier Finish. Insert metal ore blocks at Start, power Start with both fluxes, and collect raw metals at Finish. Use Silk Touch to bring ore blocks home. |
| **Smelter** | Runs furnace recipes at ten times normal speed by default. Insert through its left front slot and take results from the right. It uses both fluxes instead of furnace fuel. |
| **Recycler** | Recovers roughly 75% of the direct ingredients from Constructor products. It also accepts spare equipment modules. Supply both fluxes and leave room for every output. No blueprint is needed to recycle an item. |

The Purifier gives **two, three, four or five raw metals per ore block** with one,
two, three or four tubes respectively. Longer lines take more time and flux.
Four tubes normally need 1,600 of each flux per active tick: two tanks per
generator can sustain the line and a Smelter. With a Combiner, set its transfer
limit to cover the load, for example 2,000. Raw metals cannot be purified again.

Right-click a supported machine with an **Extreme Circuit** to install an upgrade.
It halves power use and doubles operation speed where applicable. It supports the
Smelter, Recycler, Auto Constructor, Automation Field, Forcefield Generator, Flux
Ship Charger and Purifier Start. In a Power Combiner it doubles transfer and
reduces loss to 1%. Each machine accepts one circuit. The upgrade survives breaking
and replacing the machine; recycling the machine returns the circuit.

## Storage

### Storage Cubes

A regular **Storage Cube** holds four item types; the **3×3 version** holds nine.
Each front-panel area holds 64 stacks of one type: up to 4,096 items for items
that stack to 64.

- Right-click an area with an item to insert the held stack.
- Double right-click an established area to deposit matching inventory items.
- Left-click to take one item; sneak-left-click to take up to a stack.

Unlocked cubes support item automation. A locked cube needs its matching
storage-access card.

### Holo Storage Cubes

These glass cubes hold a mixture of ordinary stacks: **30, 60 or 120**, depending
on tier. Right-click for the storage hologram. Drag stacks, click a source then a
destination, or sneak-click to transfer immediately between inventories. Side
arrows scroll through storage; **A–Z** and **Count** sort it.

They need no power, accept item automation through all six faces when unlocked,
and preserve their contents when broken. Locks block access, including automation.
Dungeon cubes need the city's storage-access card.

## Automation fields {#an-automation-field}

An **Automation Field** moves items between inventories inside a marked area,
without item pipes between every machine.

1. Place the controller and build a connected, **closed border of Field Markers**
   at its base height, touching it. Keep the interior clear for machines and nodes.
2. Set the field height to include all nodes.
3. Put an **Output Item Node** on the source inventory and an **Input Item Node**
   on the destination. Keep the nodes themselves inside the field.
4. Set filters on both nodes and supply both fluxes to the controller.
5. Start with an Output batch size of one and room in the destination.

An Output Node takes items **out of its inventory**; an Input Node puts them **into
its inventory**. An empty **whitelist allows nothing**. An empty **blacklist
allows everything**. Double-click inventory items to copy filter samples and
double-click samples to clear them.

Filters can match tags, item data and durability. Input categories such as
Smeltable, Wood and Ingots help sort a processing line; an item must pass both
the category and sample filter. Larger batches use less flux per item. A Round
Robin Node behind an Output Node distributes batches among eligible destinations.

**Sensor Item Nodes** compare the total contents of their inventory with a chosen
number and output a colored condition. Require that color on a transfer node to
build a refill system. For example, turn green when a dedicated material chest
contains fewer than your target, then require green on its supplying Output Node.
Positive conditions must be on; negative conditions must be off.

## Power armor and weapons {#modular-power-armor}

### Fuel and configuration

Wear power armor or hold an **Enhanced Energy Sabre** or **Energy Lance** in your
main hand, then press **0**. Select a piece, click its module icons to install or
remove modules, and enable the **On** checkboxes. Installing a module alone does
not activate it. Assign hotkey groups for abilities you want to toggle together.

Carry an **Energy Holder** fueled with **both Negarite and Posirite Dust**. It
powers all active equipment from one shared supply. Open its panel to move dust,
or enable Automatic mode to collect loose dust from your inventory. One matching
pair provides 1,000 equipment power. Surplus dust of only one type cannot power
anything. The holder is carried in your inventory, leaving the lance's off hand
free.

Each equipment piece has its own module budget, normally **100 usage**. An enabled
**Energy Efficiency** module raises that piece's budget to **130** and reduces its
fuel draw. Overloading a piece stops its powered abilities, even when the shared
holder still has fuel. Check fuel, activation and budgets before a fight or flight.

### Choosing armor modules

| Module | Benefit |
| --- | --- |
| Armor Upgrade | Adds armor, toughness and protection against armor-piercing lasers and magic. Four working upgrades provide 80% protection against those energy attacks. |
| Forcefield | Chest protection against ranged attacks, magic and explosions, plus reduced melee damage and retaliation. Hits trigger recharge periods with reduced protection. |
| Regeneration | Chest healing. |
| Nightvision / Scramble | Helmet night vision or protection from Sentinel detection. Scramble does not pacify other enemies. |
| Flight / Hover | Chest upward thrust with Jump, descent with Sneak, and altitude holding while Flight works. |
| Speed | Leggings movement boost. |
| Featherfalling / Step Assist | Boots protection from falling or easier movement over obstacles. |

Choose a loadout: every chest ability together exceeds the budget. Armor,
Regeneration and Forcefield fit with Energy Efficiency; Armor, Flight, Hover and
Forcefield are another option. Some modules are loot-only. A forcefield is not
protection from falling, drowning or the void; use the appropriate ability and
keep it powered.

### Sabres and Energy Lance

The ordinary **Energy Sabre** is a melee weapon. Its enhanced version supports
Power, Inhibit, Looting, Fire and Energy Efficiency modules. **Inhibit** suppresses
Master Soldier regeneration and strips beneficial effects. Sabres still use
durability.

The **Energy Lance** fires a laser with right-click, once per second. Your **off
hand must be empty**. It needs no ammunition or durability, and basic firing needs
no fuel. It accepts the same weapon modules as the enhanced sabre; those upgrades
need an Energy Holder. Blueprints and caches are the main way to obtain these
weapons; defenders only exceptionally drop what they carry.

## Transport

### Elevators

Place an **Elevator**, keep its vertical shaft clear and set its height in the
hologram. Stand in the beam and use Jump to rise or Sneak to descend. No power
supply is needed.

Add **Level Markers** at landings, within two blocks horizontally of the elevator
and inside its height. With multiple floors, Jump and Sneak select the next floor;
the beam hologram also offers floor buttons. Right-click a marker to name a floor.

### Flux Levitators

Build a continuous route of straight, bend and slope **Flux Beams**, leaving space
for the cabin and rider. Each slope rises one block. Use a Flux Levitator item on
a beam to place the cart, then right-click it to board. It needs no generator
connection.

Hold the forward movement key to accelerate in the direction you face. Facing
the other way slows the cart through zero and reverses it. The back movement key
brakes; releasing keys keeps your speed. Both end holograms offer speed controls
and an instant stop. Sneak to dismount, or sneak-right-click an empty cart to
recover it.

### Flux Ships

Place a **Flux Ship** in a clear area and right-click to board. Newly crafted ships
start **empty**. Center the stationary ship just above a **Flux Ship Charger**
connected to both flux supplies. A full charge takes about a minute and lasts
about 20 minutes at full speed; hovering uses much less.

Default controls are:

| Control | Action |
| --- | --- |
| W / S | Forward / backward thrust. Release to brake smoothly and hover. |
| Mouse look | Steer and pitch the hull; look up while moving forward to climb. |
| A / D | Turn left / right. |
| Left Alt | Look around without steering. |
| Up / Down | Add upward / downward thrust. Backspace also descends. |
| Space | Fire the front laser cannon. Aim along the cockpit crosshair. |
| Sneak | Dismount. |

Acceleration and turning take time. An empty or unattended ship gently descends.
Sneak-right-click an unoccupied ship to recover it; its charge is preserved.

## Security and forcefields

### Doors and wireless controls

**Door Markers** create automatic doors across an opening. Place them along the
floor and provide a ceiling to stop the panels at the intended height. Nearby
**Locks** control doors and storage within their configured ranges. Matching Key
Cards toggle access; forcefield-immunity cards instead need to stay in your
inventory when crossing the matching city's shield. Blank cards cannot currently
be freely programmed into personal keys through a survival interface.

For your own switch-controlled entrance, place a **Wireless Button**. Hold a
**Wireless Lock** item and right-click the button to copy its channel, then place
the lock near your doors or storage. Operate the button with an empty hand. A
**Signal Transmitter** and **Signal Receiver** can instead carry redstone signals
over a wireless channel. Keep devices loaded and use one signal source per channel.
Storage with its own security key cannot be overridden by an unrelated controller.

### Forcefield Generator {#a-powerful-forcefield}

A **Forcefield Generator** builds a spherical shield. Set its radius and redstone
mode, then provide both fluxes. Larger fields need more power: radius 10 needs
300 of each flux per tick just to stay active, with extra demand for building and
repairing panels.

Active panels hurt intruders and intercept projectiles. Attacks can damage them
and leave temporary holes. Watch **Field Integrity** and leave spare generation
for repairs. A matching city immunity card prevents that shield's contact damage;
a door or storage card does not.

## Displays and building {#other-blocks}

Join **Holographic Display Bases** side by side with the same facing to make a
shared screen, and set its height through a base's hologram. They need no power.
Choose invisible, transparent or solid backgrounds, colors and borders.

Configure a held display module, sneak-right-click its target to link it, then
right-click the display's front to install it. Drag installed modules with
right-click to rearrange them, or sneak-right-click to recover them.

- **Power, Storage, Fluid and Energy modules** show machine readings as numbers,
  meters or histories. These displays report resources; they do not transfer them.
- **Text modules** add labels and instructions. **Alien Text Modules** are
  decorative souvenirs salvaged from city displays, with no crafting blueprint.
- **Camera modules** link to placed cameras and show captured holographic scenes
  or live views. One module can select among up to four linked cameras.

Cameras only use loaded terrain. Live views currently require nearby cameras in
your dimension and shader packs switched off; they do not load distant areas.

For the surrounding base, use colored marble, bricks, pillars, slabs, slopes,
ramps, tech panels, glass and fences. Flux Glow and Flat Lights provide lighting.
A glowing panel texture does not necessarily light a room, so check dark corners
when securing a captured base.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Constructor shows no usable recipe | Put a populated blueprint in directly adjacent Blueprint Storage and carry its ingredients. Manual construction needs no cable power. |
| Powered machine stops | Check both fluxes, generator tanks, connectors, redstone mode, ingredients and output room. |
| Automation moves nothing | Close the marker border, include the nodes within its height, check both filters and conditions, and make the batch small enough. |
| Equipment ability stops | Check the Energy Holder's two fuel types, the module's On checkbox and that piece's usage budget. |
| Lance will not fire | Empty your off hand. |
| City storage rejects a card | Use storage access for that city; the final AI Core supplies it. Door access is a separate permission. |
| Ship says No power | Stop it centered directly over a Charger with both flux supplies. |
| Tablet does not open | Check whether a compatible Patchouli version is installed for your Minecraft version and loader. |
